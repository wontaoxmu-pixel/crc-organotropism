# Figure Legends & Display Item Plan

## Main figure legends

Figure 1 | Study design and consensus molecular subtype (CMS) corroboration of the peritoneal stromal niche. (A) Cohort-by-sample-type composition of the five transcriptomic cohorts and the MSK-MET clinical-genomic cohort. Tile annotations give sample counts (n). GSE190609 "Met" denotes lymph-node (n = 11) and ovarian (n = 2) metastases; GSE41568 "Met" denotes omentum (n = 4), abdominal wall (n = 1), and unspecified metastases (n = 39). For MSK-MET, 1,147 metastasis samples with organ annotation are shown, each corresponding to a distinct patient (overall-survival Cox cohort, n = 1,136). PT, primary tumor; PM, peritoneal metastasis; LM, liver metastasis; LungM, lung metastasis. (B) CMS distribution in peritoneal (n = 59) versus liver (n = 6) metastases of GSE190609 (CMSclassifier random forest, nearestCMS assignment). CMS4 accounted for 47.5% of peritoneal versus 16.7% of liver metastases; at the patient level, 9 of 12 patients had at least one CMS4 peritoneal sample (patient-level mean CMS4 fraction 52.8% in PM versus 16.7% in LM). (C) Odds ratios (OR) for CMS4 in peritoneal versus other sites. Primary estimates derive from a patient-aware generalized linear mixed model (binomial family, per-patient random intercept): PM versus PT OR = 3.72, 95% CI 1.23–11.29, P = 0.020; PM versus LM OR = 32.57, 95% CI 0.84–1,268.63, P = 0.062 (unstable; only 6 liver samples). Sample-level Fisher's exact tests on all samples (nearestCMS, n = 113) and on high-confidence assignments (minimum posterior probability ≥ 0.5, n = 74) are shown as descriptive. Points, OR; horizontal lines, 95% confidence intervals (CI); dashed line, OR = 1; x axis on log scale.

Figure 2 | Organ-specific transcriptomic niche imprints from paired cross-cohort meta-analysis. (A) Volcano plot of random-effects meta-analysis effect sizes (meta β) for peritoneal metastasis versus primary tumor (PM vs PT; GSE190609 and GSE225182; 14,085 common genes). Red, 49-gene peritoneal-tropism set (PM_core); blue, LM_core genes overlaid for reference; grey, all other genes. In total 282 genes reached FDR < 0.05 with |β| ≥ 1, of which 280 (99.3%) were sign-concordant across cohorts. Selected genes are labeled. (B) Per-gene forest plots for seven peritoneal-tropism genes: per-cohort log2 fold change (log2FC; points) with 95% CI (lines) and the random-effects pooled estimate (red diamonds). (C) As in (B), for seven liver-tropism genes across four cohorts (GSE190609, GSE50760, GSE41258, GSE41568) with the pooled estimate (blue diamonds). β denotes the REML pooled effect (metafor); FDR, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P value. Heterogeneity was low for the peritoneal program (PM_core median I² = 0%) and high for the liver program (LM_core median I² = 96.9%).

Figure 3 | Genomic background of peritoneal tropism and overall survival in the MSK-MET cohort. (A) Frequencies of non-silent driver mutations in peritoneal (intra-abdominal and ovary pooled, n = 124) versus liver (n = 624) metastasis samples (dumbbell plot; red, peritoneal; blue, liver); each metastasis sample corresponds to a distinct patient. ***FDR < 0.001 (Fisher's exact test on the same pooled peritoneal definition versus liver, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted across 18 genes). (B) Patient-level enrichment of mutations in patients with peritoneal involvement (n = 124) versus those without (n = 1,023). Points, odds ratios from Fisher's exact test; lines, 95% CI; x axis on log scale; **FDR < 0.01 (BH across 9 genes). Red, significant enrichment with peritoneal involvement; blue, significant depletion; grey, not significant. (C) Overall-survival Cox proportional-hazards models in the subset with complete metastatic-burden annotation (n = 405; 226 events). Filled black points, burden-adjusted model; open grey points, the same subset without the burden term. Lines, 95% CI; x axis on log scale; dashed line, HR = 1. Peritoneal involvement was non-significant both without (P = 0.181) and with (P = 0.102) the burden term, with an undiminished point estimate, indicating loss of power in the smaller subset rather than attenuation by burden; the full-cohort model without the burden term (n = 1,136; HR = 1.42, P = 0.022) is referenced in the text only. Overall survival (OS_MONTHS) is defined from the date of sequencing and is subject to left truncation.

Figure 4 | Limits of primary-tumor prediction and hepatocyte contamination of liver-metastasis transcriptomes. (A) Apparent versus leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) sensitivity discrimination of metastatic destination from primary-tumor single-sample gene-set scores, with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (2,000 resamples). Apparent: LM_core AUC = 0.933 (95% CI 0.845–0.996); PM_core AUC = 0.719 (95% CI 0.560–0.859). LOCO main row: AUC = 0.736 (95% CI 0.598–0.863); detectable-gene restricted subset: AUC = 0.596 (95% CI 0.441–0.734); cross-cohort PM_core: AUC = 0.532 (95% CI 0.351–0.716). Against 1,000 size- and detection-rate–matched random gene sets, only the apparent LM_core row marginally exceeded the null (empirical P = 0.031; apparent PM_core P = 0.302; LOCO main row P = 0.223; restricted subset P = 0.367; the cross-cohort configuration's random-set mean was itself 0.600), so primary-tumor prediction is not reliably supported at the current sample sizes. Dashed line, chance (AUC = 0.5). NS, not significant. (B) Percentage of tropism genes retained after epithelial-fraction adjustment in GSE190609 (PM_core, 34/49, 69%; LM_core, 57/57, 100%; the 100% LM_core retention was independently reproduced in GSE50760, 57/57, not shown). (C) Hepatocyte contamination: log2 fold change of liver-specific markers (ALB, APOA1, HP, APOB, SERPINA1) in liver metastases versus primary tumors across three cohorts (shapes). Dashed line, log2FC = 2 (typical differential-expression magnitude). AUC, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve.
