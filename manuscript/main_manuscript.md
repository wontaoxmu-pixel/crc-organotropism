# Abstract

**Background:** Colorectal cancer (CRC) metastasizes preferentially to the liver and peritoneum, yet the molecular basis of this organotropism remains incompletely defined, and whether the primary-tumor transcriptome encodes metastatic destination is unresolved.

**Methods:** We pooled patient-matched comparisons across five public gene-expression cohorts to derive a peritoneal-tropism gene set (PM_core) and a liver-tropism gene set (LM_core). Consensus molecular subtypes (CMS) were assigned in a multi-organ RNA-seq cohort, with per-patient modeling of subtype enrichment. Genomic associations of peritoneal versus liver spread were tested in 3,548 CRC samples from MSK-MET. Whether primary-tumor expression predicts metastatic site was evaluated by single-sample gene-set scoring, restricting validation to cohorts not used for gene-set derivation and comparing performance against size-matched random gene sets.

**Results:** Peritoneal metastases showed a stromal/adipocytic niche imprint (PM_core, 49 genes, including *FABP4* and *PI16*), with CMS4 enrichment confirmed as a positive control under patient-aware modeling (OR = 3.72, 95% CI 1.23–11.29, *P* = 0.020 versus primary tumors). Liver metastases carried a hepatocyte-associated program (LM_core, 57 genes). Peritoneal spread tracked an APC-depleted genomic background: *APC* mutations were depleted at the metastasis-sample level (64.5% vs 82.2%; OR = 0.39, FDR = 5.1 × 10⁻⁴), with a nominal *SMAD4* enrichment that did not survive multiple-testing correction. Primary-tumor expression of these gene sets could not be distinguished from size-matched random gene sets under leave-one-cohort-out validation at the current sample sizes. The liver-tropism signal was quantitatively consistent with substantial hepatocyte admixture.

**Conclusions:** Metastatic lesions retain organ-specific transcriptomic niche imprints, and peritoneal spread tracks an APC-depleted genomic background. The primary-tumor transcriptome was not reliably supported as a predictor of metastatic destination, and bulk liver-metastasis transcriptomes require routine control for hepatocyte contamination.

**Keywords:** colorectal cancer; peritoneal metastasis; liver metastasis; organotropism; consensus molecular subtypes; APC; hepatocyte contamination; transcriptomics

# Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide, and most deaths are attributable to metastatic disease rather than the primary tumor. Among metastatic sites, the liver and the peritoneum follow substantially different clinical courses. Liver metastases are frequently amenable to surgical resection or local ablation and generally retain sensitivity to systemic chemotherapy, whereas peritoneal metastasis (PM) is associated with poorer overall survival and attenuated responses to systemic chemotherapy, and curative-intent cytoreductive surgery with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (CRS+HIPEC) is applicable to only selected patients [1,2] — and the incremental benefit of HIPEC over cytoreduction alone has been questioned by the PRODIGE 7 trial [3]. Why circulating tumor cells colonize one organ rather than another — the phenomenon of metastatic organotropism — remains incompletely understood in CRC, and this gap limits both prognostication and the rational design of site-directed surveillance and therapy.

Several lines of evidence point to molecular correlates of metastatic destination. The consensus molecular subtype (CMS) framework established that CRC is transcriptionally heterogeneous [4], and peritoneal metastases have been consistently assigned to the mesenchymal CMS4 subtype [5,6]. Separately, *BRAF*-mutant, serrated-type tumors show proximal predominance and an increased propensity for peritoneal seeding [7], and among peritoneal metastases *BRAF* mutations co-occur with ligand-dependent, non-canonical WNT alterations (*RNF43*–*RSPO*) [2], suggesting a genomic background that may favor peritoneal spread. At the copy-number and cytogenetic level, Golas and colleagues recently mapped organotropic patterns across metastatic sites [8]. What is still missing is a transcriptome-level, multi-organ comparison — ideally within the same patients — that includes the peritoneum: most existing expression studies contrast primary tumors with liver metastases alone, leaving peritoneal deposits largely uncharacterized relative to other sites.

Two methodological questions further complicate this landscape. First, bulk transcriptomes of liver metastases can be confounded by admixed hepatocyte-derived transcripts, so that a "liver-tropism" expression signal may partly reflect residual organ-resident cells rather than tumor-intrinsic programming: organ-specific genes must be excluded before liver-metastasis signatures are derived [9], the liver microenvironment infiltrates and shapes metastatic transcriptomes [10], and normal-cell admixture is a recognized confounder of tumor expression signatures [11]; whether published liver-metastasis signatures survive explicit control for this effect is unclear, and epithelial-content adjustment alone may be insufficient to remove signal from organ-resident cells. Second, the notion that the primary tumor is pre-programmed for a specific metastatic destination — which would enable primary-tumor–based prediction of spread and, potentially, risk-adapted surveillance — is supported by some retrospective analyses [12,13] but disputed by others [14], and few studies have tested it under leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) cross-cohort validation, where information leakage between signature derivation and evaluation can be excluded.

Here we show that metastatic CRC lesions retain organ-specific transcriptomic niche imprints — a stromal/adipocytic program in peritoneal metastases and a hepatocyte-derived signal in liver metastases — and that peritoneal spread is associated with an APC-depleted genomic background, while the primary-tumor transcriptome does not reliably encode metastatic destination. We combined paired multi-organ RNA-sequencing cohorts from five GEO datasets in a random-effects meta-analysis with an independent clinical-genomic cohort of 3,548 CRC samples from MSK-MET (Memorial Sloan Kettering–Metastatic Events and Tropisms), and we subjected the predictive claims to LOCO sensitivity analyses and random-gene-set permutation baselines. Peritoneal metastases showed a predefined stromal/adipocytic peritoneal-tropism gene set (PM_core), corroborated by CMS4 enrichment that we treat as a positive control, because the same association was previously reported for this cohort [6]. Peritoneal involvement was associated with *APC* depletion, with a nominal *SMAD4* enrichment that did not survive multiple-testing correction; although prior literature suggests a serrated-type, WNT-ligand–dependent context [2,7], this framework was not supported by our genomic data. In contrast, apparent primary-tumor prediction of metastatic site could not be distinguished from size-matched random gene sets, and the liver-tropism signal was quantitatively consistent with substantial hepatocyte admixture. Together, these findings outline a candidate framework for the organotropism of CRC that merits prospective and experimental validation, and they delineate where primary-tumor–based prediction and bulk liver-metastasis transcriptomics should — and should not — be trusted.

## Methods

### Data sources and cohorts

Five colorectal cancer (CRC) cohorts were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). GSE190609 [6] (RNA-seq, RPKM) comprised 113 samples from 12 patients, including 35 primary tumor (PT) regions, 59 peritoneal metastases (PM), 6 liver metastases (LM), and 13 other metastatic sites (11 lymph nodes and 2 ovarian metastases); all 12 patients had peritoneal involvement. GSE50760 [15] (RNA-seq, FPKM) comprised 54 samples from 18 patients (18 matched normal colon, PT, and LM trios). GSE225182 [16] (RNA-seq, log2CPM) comprised 33 samples (20 PT, 7 PM, and 6 normal colon), of which 7 matched PT–PM pairs were used for paired analysis. GSE41258 [17] (microarray) comprised 186 PT, 47 LM, 20 lung metastases (LungM), and 54 normal colon samples, and GSE41568 [18] (microarray) comprised 133 samples (80 LM, 8 LungM, 1 PM, 4 omental, 1 abdominal wall, and 39 unannotated). As a patient-matched structure could not be established, GSE41258 and GSE41568 served as reference cohorts rather than paired discovery cohorts. The Memorial Sloan Kettering–Metastatic Events and Tropisms (MSK-MET) cohort [19] was exported from cBioPortal [20,21]: 3,548 CRC samples were analyzed, including 1,147 metastasis samples with organ-level annotation that corresponded to 1,147 distinct patients (strictly one metastasis sample per patient, so sample-level genomic analyses are free of within-patient pseudo-replication); 1,136 patients were evaluable for survival analysis, 405 formed the metastatic-burden–annotated subset, and 124 with versus 1,023 without peritoneal involvement formed the patient-level comparison. All data were de-identified and publicly available; institutional ethics review was exempted.

### Expression processing and paired differential expression

Normalized expression matrices were used as released by the original studies (RPKM for GSE190609, FPKM for GSE50760, and log2CPM for GSE225182) and were log2-transformed where required. For the microarray cohorts, series-matrix probe intensities were mapped to gene symbols using the corresponding platform annotation packages, and probes mapping to the same gene were collapsed by retaining the probe with the highest mean expression. In GSE190609, differential expression among organs was estimated with paired limma with patient blocking [22], using a design matrix of the form ~ organ + patient and contrasts for PM versus PT, LM versus PT, and PM versus LM; the same blocked design was applied to the 7 matched pairs of GSE225182 and to the 18 matched PT–LM pairs of GSE50760. Microarray cohorts were analyzed with unpaired limma models comparing each metastatic site with PT.

### Cross-cohort random-effects meta-analysis

For each contrast, per-cohort log2 fold-changes and their variances (derived from limma moderated *t* statistics) were combined across cohorts with random-effects meta-analysis using restricted maximum-likelihood estimation (REML) in metafor [23]. Microarray and RNA-seq cohorts were analyzed as platform strata before pooling, and platform-stratified results are reported in the Supplementary Material. Genes were required to show a consistent direction of effect across contributing cohorts. The peritoneal meta-analysis combined GSE190609 and GSE225182 (14,085 common genes), and the liver meta-analysis combined GSE190609, GSE50760, GSE41258, and GSE41568 (9,333 common genes). Organ-tropism gene sets were defined as genes with meta-analysis FDR < 0.05, |β| ≥ 1, and cross-cohort direction concordance, intersected with genes showing a concordant direction in the GSE190609 PM versus LM comparison; this yielded the peritoneal-tropism gene set (PM_core, 49 genes) and the liver-tropism gene set (LM_core, 57 genes). For the peritoneal meta-analysis, only two RNA-seq cohorts (GSE190609 and GSE225182) were available. With k = 2 studies, between-study variance (τ²) estimated by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) is notoriously imprecise, and heterogeneity statistics (τ², I², Cochran's Q) should be interpreted as descriptive rather than inferential. We therefore (i) additionally report fixed-effect model estimates as a sensitivity analysis, (ii) performed leave-one-cohort-out analyses, which for k = 2 reduce to single-cohort re-analysis, and (iii) required sign concordance across cohorts for inclusion in the PM_core signature, so that no gene enters the signature on the strength of a single cohort.

### Consensus molecular subtype classification

Consensus molecular subtypes (CMS) were assigned in GSE190609 with the random-forest classifier of CMSclassifier [4], with 272 of 273 model genes covered after Entrez identifier mapping. Two classification schemes were applied: nearestCMS calls for all 113 samples, and a stringent version retaining only samples with a maximum posterior probability (minPosterior) ≥ 0.5 (*n* = 74). Because multiple samples derive from the same patient, the primary test of the association between CMS4 and metastatic organ was a generalized linear mixed model (GLMM; binomial family, per-patient random intercept (1|patient)) fitted with lme4 1.1.30; the model converged. A sample-level Fisher's exact test is reported as descriptive only, and a duplicateCorrelation-based analysis was performed as a sensitivity check.

### Single-sample scoring and leave-one-cohort-out sensitivity analysis

PT samples from all five cohorts (*n* = 293) were scored for PM_core and LM_core using rank-based single-sample gene-set scoring, implemented as a custom within-sample rank Kolmogorov–Smirnov random-walk statistic with cross-sample range normalization, as described by Barbie et al. [24]; a custom implementation was used because of GSVA version-compatibility constraints, and the full scoring script is publicly released. The cross-sample range normalization is an order-preserving affine transformation and therefore does not affect rank-based discrimination: strictly per-sample and cross-sample–normalized implementations yielded identical per-sample rankings (Spearman ρ = 1.000) and identical Mann–Whitney AUCs (ΔAUC = 0 across all six scoring configurations), and an independent GSEApy 1.3.1 (Python) implementation reproduced the primary tests with consistent direction (AUC 0.903 for LM_core and 0.716 for PM_core, versus 0.933 and 0.719 with the primary implementation). Discrimination between PT of patients with liver versus peritoneal metastasis was quantified by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) computed from the Mann–Whitney statistic, with two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum *P* values and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (2,000 resamples). As a null reference, 1,000 random gene sets matched for size and detection rate were scored under each configuration, yielding an empirical *P* value for the observed AUC. To remove circularity between gene-set derivation and scoring, leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) sensitivity analyses were performed with gene sets derived exclusively from cohorts not represented among the scored samples: LM_core was re-derived from the microarray cohorts only (21 genes) and scored on RNA-seq PT, and PM_core was re-derived from GSE190609 only (91 genes) and scored on independent-cohort PT. A sensitivity subset restricted the gene sets to genes detectable in both comparison groups to exclude cross-platform rank shifts driven by undetected genes.

### MSK-MET genomic and survival analyses

At the sample level, non-silent driver mutations in metastasis samples were compared between the peritoneal compartment (intra-abdominal and ovary, pooled; *n* = 124) and liver (*n* = 624) with Fisher's exact tests across 18 driver genes, with Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) correction; as noted above, each metastasis sample corresponded to a distinct patient. A sensitivity analysis excluded ovarian metastases (intra-abdominal only, *n* = 81). Multivariable logistic regression with peritoneal (versus liver) metastasis as the outcome adjusted for age and sex (model 1; *n* = 887, of which 122 peritoneal) and, in a smaller subset, additionally for microsatellite-instability (MSI) status, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and primary-tumor sidedness (model 2; *n* = 302); MSI, TMB, and sidedness annotations derive from a sample-level snapshot with incomplete coverage and were therefore unavailable for the full cohort. At the patient level, mutation frequencies were compared between patients with (*n* = 124) and without (*n* = 1,023) peritoneal involvement using Fisher's exact tests across 9 prespecified genes with BH correction. Overall survival (OS_MONTHS) is defined by MSK-MET from the date of sequencing; because sequencing occurred after metastatic diagnosis (median interval from first metastatic diagnosis to sequencing 0.9 years, IQR 0.2–2.5; *n* = 1,125), these analyses are subject to left truncation (immortal-time bias). Survival was analyzed with Cox proportional-hazards models (*n* = 1,136; 524 events) including peritoneal, liver, and lung involvement, *BRAF* mutation status, and age at sequencing; in the subset with complete metastatic-burden annotation (*n* = 405; 226 events), models with and without the number of metastatic sites were fitted to the same samples, so that the effect of burden adjustment could be distinguished from loss of power. Copy-number alteration analysis was restricted to 177 samples and was considered exploratory.

### Purity adjustment and external-signature comparison

To quantify confounding by tumor purity, an epithelial-content proxy (mean log2 expression of *EPCAM*, *KRT8*, and *KRT19*) was added as a covariate to the GSE190609 linear model (~ organ + patient + epithelial score). The retention rate was defined as the proportion of genes significant before adjustment that remained significant after adjustment; for LM_core the retention analysis was repeated in GSE50760. Organ-resident cell admixture was further quantified with four prespecified marker sets (12 liver, 8 adipocyte, 5 mesothelial, and 3 epithelial genes): per-sample marker scores were used to attribute gene-set signals to resident-cell compartments, and the association between gene-level LM_core effect sizes and liver specificity was tested by Spearman's rank correlation. The cellular composition of PM_core was annotated against the same marker sets as an asymmetric control. Overlaps between the core sets and published signatures were tested by hypergeometric tests against the universe of genes detectable in each meta-analysis (14,085 for PM; 9,333 for LM), with a sensitivity analysis at a universe of 20,000 genes that left all conclusions unchanged.

### Statistics and reproducibility

Analyses were performed in R 4.2.0 (2022-04-22; x86_64-apple-darwin17.0, Bioconductor 3.15) using data.table 1.14.4, readxl 1.4.3, limma 3.52.2, metafor 5.2.1, survival 3.5.7, jsonlite 1.8.8, CMSclassifier 1.0.0, org.Hs.eg.db 3.15.0, AnnotationDbi 1.58.0, hgu133a.db 3.13.0, and hgu133plus2.db 3.13.0; generalized linear mixed models were fitted with lme4 1.1.30; figures were produced with ggplot2 3.4.4, patchwork 1.1.3, scales 1.2.1, and ggrepel 0.9.4. Single-sample gene-set scoring used a custom implementation of the rank-based ssGSEA statistic of Barbie et al. because of GSVA version-compatibility constraints, with an independent benchmark in GSEApy 1.3.1 (Python). All tests were two-sided, and false-discovery rates were controlled by the BH method unless otherwise stated. The random seed was fixed (set.seed(123)) in all stochastic steps. All analysis scripts are publicly available [Evidence needed: repository URL].

# Results

## Cohort overview and CMS4 enrichment in peritoneal metastases

Peritoneal metastases (PM) were enriched for the mesenchymal consensus molecular subtype 4 (CMS4) across a paired multi-organ transcriptomic cohort. Five public colorectal cancer (CRC) cohorts with matched or multi-site sampling were assembled for transcriptomic analyses, together with the clinical-genomic MSK-MET (Memorial Sloan Kettering–Metastatic Events and Tropisms) cohort [19] (Fig. 1A). GSE190609 comprised 113 RNA-seq samples from 12 patients, including 35 primary tumor (PT) regions, 59 PM, 6 liver metastases (LM), and 13 other metastatic sites (11 lymph node and 2 ovarian); all 12 patients harbored peritoneal disease, so no pure-liver control patients were available in this cohort. GSE50760 provided 54 samples from 18 patients, each with matched normal mucosa, PT, and LM. GSE225182 contributed 33 samples (20 PT, 7 PM, and 6 normal), of which 7 matched PT–PM pairs were used in paired meta-analysis. Two microarray cohorts, GSE41258 (186 PT, 47 LM, 20 lung metastases [LungM], and 54 normal) and GSE41568 (133 samples, including 80 LM), lacked a valid paired structure and were retained as reference cohorts. For genomic and survival analyses, the CRC subset of MSK-MET comprised 3,548 samples, of which 1,147 metastatic samples carried organ-level annotation and corresponded to 1,147 distinct patients (strictly one metastasis sample per patient); 124 patients had peritoneal involvement and 1,023 did not.

CMS were assigned with the random-forest CMSclassifier (272 of 273 model genes covered) using nearestCMS calls (Fig. 1B). Because multiple samples derive from the same patient, the primary test was a patient-aware generalized linear mixed model (GLMM; binomial family, per-patient random intercept; converged). CMS4 assignment was more frequent in PM than in PT (OR = 3.72, 95% CI 1.23–11.29, *P* = 0.020; 94 samples from 12 patients) and in PM than in LM (OR = 32.57, 95% CI 0.84–1,268.63, *P* = 0.062; 65 samples from 12 patients), the latter estimate being unstable because only 6 LM samples were available (the LM-versus-PT comparison comprised 41 samples from 12 patients, the 6 LM samples deriving from 6 distinct patients). At the patient level, 9 of 12 patients had at least one CMS4 PM sample, and the patient-level mean CMS4 fraction was 52.8% in PM versus 16.7% in LM; at the sample level, CMS4 accounted for 47.5% (28/59) of PM versus 16.7% (1/6) of LM (Fig. 1B). A sample-level Fisher's exact test, which ignores within-patient correlation, is reported as descriptive only (OR = 3.13, 95% CI 1.30–7.89, *P* = 0.006) (Fig. 1C); a duplicateCorrelation-based sensitivity analysis was directionally concordant with the primary analysis (Jaccard = 0.904). When classification was restricted to high-confidence calls (minPosterior ≥ 0.5, *n* = 74), the direction of the association was preserved (OR = 2.63, 95% CI 0.92–7.88, *P* = 0.060). We treat this enrichment as a positive control rather than a novel finding: it re-derives, under patient-aware modeling, the CMS4–peritoneum association previously reported for this same cohort [6] and independently in an external series [5], confirming that the pipeline recovers a known signal.

## Organ-specific niche imprints from paired meta-analysis

Paired differential expression followed by random-effects meta-analysis defined two non-overlapping organ-specific transcriptomic programs: a stromal/adipocytic program in PM and a hepatic functional program in LM. Within GSE190609, paired limma with patient blocking identified 861 differentially expressed genes for PM versus PT, 534 for LM versus PT, and 1,053 for PM versus LM (487 upregulated, 566 downregulated) at FDR < 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 1 (Supplementary Fig. S2). As a biological sanity check, PM showed upregulation of mesenchymal markers (*SPARC*, *CDH2*, *FN1*, *FAP*, *COL3A1*) with downregulation of epithelial markers (*EPCAM*, *CDH1*, *MUC1*), consistent with an epithelial–mesenchymal transition–like shift.

Random-effects meta-analysis (REML, metafor) was then performed across cohorts with valid paired contrasts. The PM program (GSE190609 and GSE225182; 14,085 shared genes) comprised 282 genes at FDR < 0.05 and |β| ≥ 1, of which 280 (99.3%) were direction-concordant across cohorts. The LM program (GSE190609, GSE50760, GSE41258, and GSE41568; 9,333 shared genes) comprised 146 significant genes, of which 145 were direction-concordant. Intersecting the meta-significant concordant genes with the same-direction PM-versus-LM contrast in GSE190609 yielded a peritoneal-tropism gene set (PM_core, 49 genes) and a liver-tropism gene set (LM_core, 57 genes) (Fig. 2A).

PM_core was dominated by stromal and adipocytic lineage genes, including *FABP4* (β = 3.43, FDR < 0.001), *PI16* (β = 2.35), *CD36* (β = 1.90), *PLAC9* (β = 1.55), *ADAMTS15* (β = 1.55), *RBP7* (β = 1.52), and *LPL* (β = 1.28) (all FDR < 0.001), together with *CADM3*, *SRPX*, and *MEOX2* (Fig. 2B). In contrast, LM_core consisted largely of hepatocyte functional and acute-phase genes, including *FGG* (β = 6.93, FDR = 0.003), *HP* (β = 5.84, FDR = 0.001), *APOC3* (β = 5.79, FDR = 0.004), *GC* (β = 5.75, FDR = 0.020), *APOA1* (β = 5.17, FDR = 0.050), *F9* (β = 3.71, FDR = 0.045), and *SERPINA1* (β = 2.22, FDR = 0.014), together with *FGA*, *CPB2*, *ORM1*, and *C9* (Fig. 2C). The LM program was largely undetectable in the PM-versus-PT comparison (only a minority of the 57 genes reached significance), indicating that the two organ-specific programs do not cross over and supporting their specificity.

Heterogeneity was low for the peritoneal program and high for the liver program. Across PM meta-analysis genes the median τ² was 0 (64.2% of genes), median I² was 0%, and Cochran's Q was significant for 7.1% of genes; among the 49 PM_core genes, median τ² = 0, median I² = 0%, and Q was significant for only 1 gene (*KCNA1*). Fixed-effect and random-effect estimates were near-identical for PM (β correlation 0.988) but diverged for LM (0.766). In leave-one-cohort-out analysis, PM_core direction was 100% concordant across the two cohorts, and in GSE225182 alone 38 of 49 PM_core genes had |log2FC| ≥ 1 (median 1.264, versus 1.426 in GSE190609); that 0 of 49 reached FDR significance in GSE225182 reflects the limited power of its 7 pairs. In contrast, the LM meta-analysis was highly heterogeneous (median I² = 96.9%; Q significant for 54 of 57 LM_core genes), consistent with platform- and admixture-driven instability of the liver signal (see contamination analyses below).

## Genomic background of peritoneal tropism and survival

In MSK-MET, peritoneal spread was associated with depletion of *APC* mutations, whereas *SMAD4* and *RNF43* showed only nominal trends; peritoneal involvement was associated with shorter overall survival in the full cohort, an association that lost significance in the smaller burden-annotated subset. Each of the 1,147 organ-annotated metastasis samples corresponded to a distinct patient, so sample-level comparisons are not subject to within-patient pseudo-replication. At the sample level (peritoneal = intra-abdominal plus ovary pooled, *n* = 124, versus liver, *n* = 624; 18 driver genes, BH-corrected), *APC* mutations were depleted in peritoneal lesions (64.5% versus 82.2%; OR = 0.394, *P* = 2.86 × 10⁻⁵, FDR = 5.1 × 10⁻⁴) (Fig. 3A). The association was stronger when ovarian metastases were excluded (intra-abdominal only, *n* = 81: *APC* 47/81 [58.0%]; OR = 0.300, 95% CI 0.179–0.504, *P* = 2.98 × 10⁻⁶, FDR = 8.94 × 10⁻⁶). In the same intra-abdominal-only analysis, *SMAD4* was not significant (OR = 1.601, *P* = 0.092), and the *RNF43* enrichment was driven by ovarian samples (intra-abdominal-only *P* = 0.121, nonsignificant). *BRAF* mutations were not enriched in peritoneal lesions (7.4%; FDR = 0.298), and neither *SMAD4* (24.7% versus 17.0%) nor *KRAS* (56.8% versus 41.3%) differed significantly at the sample level.

The *APC* depletion was independent of available clinical covariates. In multivariable logistic regression with peritoneal (versus liver) metastasis as the outcome, adjustment for age and sex left the association essentially unchanged and independently significant (model 1: *n* = 887, 122 peritoneal; *APC* OR = 0.379, 95% CI 0.249–0.584, *P* = 8.17 × 10⁻⁶). In the smaller subset with MSI, tumor mutational burden, and primary-sidedness annotation (model 2: *n* = 302), the direction was preserved but the model was underpowered (*APC* OR = 0.544, *P* = 0.140); right-sided primary location was independently enriched for peritoneal spread (OR = 2.56, 95% CI 1.18–5.49, *P* = 0.016), and MSI-high status (only 2 cases) was not evaluable.

At the patient level (124 patients with peritoneal involvement versus 1,023 without; Benjamini–Hochberg correction across 9 genes), the *APC* association was preserved (OR = 0.46, 95% CI 0.31–0.71, *P* = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴, FDR = 0.003) (Fig. 3B). *SMAD4* mutations were nominally enriched in patients with peritoneal involvement (OR = 1.62, 95% CI 0.98–2.60, *P* = 0.048) but did not remain significant after multiple-testing correction (FDR = 0.214). *RNF43* mutations showed a non-significant trend toward enrichment (8.1% versus 5.0%; OR = 1.67, 95% CI 0.74–3.45, *P* = 0.142).

Overall survival (OS_MONTHS) is defined by MSK-MET from the date of sequencing (see Methods). In a multivariable Cox model without the metastatic-burden term across the full cohort (*n* = 1,136; 524 events), peritoneal involvement was associated with worse survival (hazard ratio [HR] = 1.42, 95% CI 1.05–1.92, *P* = 0.022), as was *BRAF* mutation (HR = 1.72, 95% CI 1.29–2.31, *P* < 0.001), whereas lung involvement was associated with better survival (HR = 0.60, 95% CI 0.43–0.83, *P* = 0.002) (Fig. 3C). To separate burden adjustment from loss of power, we fitted models with and without the burden term to the same annotated subset (*n* = 405; 226 events): peritoneal involvement was already non-significant in the subset model without the burden term (HR = 1.38, 95% CI 0.86–2.22, *P* = 0.181) and remained so after adding the number of metastatic sites (HR = 1.49, 95% CI 0.92–2.39, *P* = 0.102). The point estimate did not decrease — the log HR increased — indicating that the loss of significance reflects reduced power in the smaller subset rather than metastatic burden explaining the prognostic signal. The number of metastatic sites was itself strongly associated with survival (HR = 1.08 per site, 95% CI 1.05–1.11, *P* = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸). The adverse prognostic association of *BRAF* mutation remained significant after burden adjustment (HR = 1.76, 95% CI 1.16–2.68, *P* = 0.008). Copy-number analyses were limited to 177 samples and yielded no credible organ-specific signal; these exploratory results are reported in the Supplementary Information.

## The primary-tumor transcriptome does not reliably encode metastatic destination

The apparent ability of primary-tumor transcriptomes to predict metastatic destination did not survive leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) sensitivity analyses and permutation baselines. When PM_core and LM_core scores were computed in 293 primary tumors across the five transcriptomic cohorts using a rank-based ssGSEA implementation, LM_core scores distinguished primary tumors of patients with liver metastasis from those of patients with peritoneal metastasis with an apparent area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.933 (bootstrap 95% CI 0.845–0.996, *P* = 4.5 × 10⁻⁸), and PM_core scores achieved an apparent AUC of 0.719 (95% CI 0.560–0.859, *P* = 0.006) (Fig. 4A). However, because the gene sets were partly derived from the same cohorts in which they were evaluated, these estimates are subject to circularity and to cross-platform rank shifts.

LOCO sensitivity analyses were therefore performed. When LM_core was re-derived exclusively from the microarray cohorts (21 detectable genes) and applied to RNA-seq primary tumors, discrimination remained nominally significant (AUC = 0.736, 95% CI 0.598–0.863, *P* = 0.003); however, when the analysis was further restricted to genes detectable across both platforms and patient groups, the AUC fell to 0.596 (*P* = 0.229) and was no longer significant. PM_core, which could be derived only from GSE190609 (91 genes), showed no cross-cohort discrimination (AUC = 0.532, *P* = 0.753).

To test whether these discriminations exceed what any comparably sized gene set would achieve under the same cohort structure, we scored 1,000 random gene sets matched for size and detection rate under each configuration (bootstrap 95% CIs, 2,000 resamples). The apparent LM_core discrimination only marginally exceeded this null (random-set mean AUC = 0.489; empirical *P* = 0.031), and no other configuration did: apparent PM_core, *P* = 0.302; LOCO main row, *P* = 0.223; LOCO restricted subset, *P* = 0.367; and for the cross-cohort configuration the random-set mean was itself 0.600, indicating that batch structure between cohorts inflates the null baseline. Thus, apart from the marginally significant apparent LM_core row, no configuration can be distinguished from size-matched random gene sets at the current sample sizes. The apparent predictive signal was therefore driven by circular gene-set derivation and platform-dependent rank structure, and primary-tumor prediction of metastatic destination is not reliably supported — rather than disproven — at the current sample sizes; we note that one LOCO configuration (microarray-derived LM_core applied to RNA-seq primaries) retained nominal significance, so this conclusion rests on the restricted detectable-gene sensitivity subset and the permutation baseline rather than on uniform failure across all settings.

Because bulk metastatic samples contain organ-resident cells, we next asked whether the organ-specific programs could be explained by contamination rather than tumor-intrinsic adaptation. In GSE190609, adding an epithelial-fraction covariate to the paired models retained 51% of PM-versus-PT and 73% of PM-versus-LM differential genes; 34 of 49 PM_core genes (69%) remained significant after adjustment, whereas all 57 LM_core genes (100%) were retained (Fig. 4B), and the 100% LM_core retention was independently reproduced in GSE50760 (57/57, 100%). We note that GSE50760 contributes to the LM meta-analysis and is therefore not a fully held-out cohort, and that the EPCAM-based proxy does not capture hepatocyte content, so retention is compatible with — rather than evidence against — admixture. An epithelial-fraction covariate therefore does not account for organ-resident cell admixture. Quantitative attribution analyses supported hepatocyte admixture as the origin of the liver signal: 56 of 57 LM_core genes are known liver-function genes; gene-level LM_core effect sizes correlated with liver specificity (Spearman ρ = 0.635, *P* = 1.1 × 10⁻⁷); and attribution against a 12-gene liver marker score was quantitatively consistent with substantial hepatocyte admixture. PM_core showed an asymmetric pattern: 10 of 49 genes annotated to adipocyte/lipid handling and 16 of 49 to fibroblast/ECM identity, with no mesothelial markers (0/49); an adipocyte marker score attributed approximately 97% of the PM_core signal but to mixed niche components, indicating that PM_core does not reflect contamination by a single resident cell type. Hepatocyte markers were strongly elevated in LM across RNA-seq cohorts (LM versus PT log2 fold change for *ALB*: 10.98 in GSE190609, 6.11 in GSE50760, and 0.19 in GSE41258; *APOB*: 8.24, 4.95, and 0.69; *APOA1*: 9.03, 7.78, and 0.57; *HP*: 8.88, 7.98, and 2.85; *SERPINA1*: 2.60, 4.08, and 0.99) (Fig. 4C). The attenuated fold changes in the microarray cohort GSE41258 reflect platform compression rather than absence of contamination. Together, these data indicate that the liver-tropism signal in bulk LM transcriptomes is quantitatively consistent with substantial admixture of hepatocyte transcripts, whereas the peritoneal stromal/adipocytic imprint partially survives epithelial-fraction adjustment and is not attributable to a single resident cell type. Accordingly, transcriptomic scores from these gene sets should not be used to anticipate metastatic destination from primary tumors without prospective validation.

## Discussion

In this study, we combined a paired multi-organ transcriptomic meta-analysis of five GEO cohorts with the 3,548-sample Memorial Sloan Kettering–Metastatic Events and Tropisms (MSK-MET) clinical-genomic cohort [19] to examine whether colorectal cancer (CRC) metastases carry organ-specific molecular features. Two lines of evidence emerged. Peritoneal metastases (PM) showed a stromal/adipocytic niche imprint — a 49-gene peritoneal-tropism set (PM_core) enriched for lipid-handling and mesenchymal genes such as *FABP4*, *CD36*, and *PI16* — corroborated by enrichment of the CMS4 mesenchymal subtype under patient-aware modeling (GLMM OR = 3.72, 95% CI 1.23–11.29, *P* = 0.020), which we treat as a positive control because the association was previously reported for the same cohort [6]. Liver metastases (LM) showed a reciprocal 57-gene liver-tropism set (LM_core) enriched for hepatic function and acute-phase genes, and the two organ programs were essentially silent in each other's comparisons, indicating organ specificity rather than a generic metastatic program. In parallel, peritoneal spread was associated with an APC-depleted genomic background (patient-level OR = 0.46, 95% CI 0.31–0.71, *P* = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴), accompanied by a nominal *SMAD4* enrichment that did not survive multiple-testing correction. Two additional findings bound the interpretation: primary-tumor prediction of metastatic destination was not reliably supported — no scoring configuration exceeded size-matched random gene-set baselines beyond a marginal signal in the apparent LM_core analysis — and the apparent liver-tropism transcriptional program was quantitatively consistent with substantial hepatocyte admixture rather than with tumor-intrinsic biology.

The peritoneal imprint is biologically plausible. PM_core is dominated by genes of adipocytic lipid handling (*FABP4*, *CD36*, *LPL*, *RBP7*) and stromal/mesenchymal identity (*PI16*, *PLAC9*, *ADAMTS15*), a composition consistent with the mesothelial and adipose-rich microenvironment of the peritoneal cavity and omentum [25,26] and with adipocyte–tumor FABP4 coupling described in peritoneal-seeding malignancies [27]. The CMS4 enrichment in PM is likewise consistent with the reported association between the mesenchymal consensus subtype and peritoneal dissemination [5,6], and its direction was preserved under a stricter subtype-calling threshold (OR = 2.63, 95% CI 0.92–7.88, *P* = 0.060) and under patient-aware modeling. Because CMS4 classification partly reflects stromal admixture, this convergence does not by itself localize the imprint to tumor cells; however, the PM_core signal was largely preserved after adjustment for epithelial fraction (34 of 49 genes) and comprised mixed niche components rather than a single resident cell type, indicating that it is not simply a consequence of reduced tumor cellularity or of one contaminating population. Prior literature would frame the accompanying genomic background within a serrated-type, WNT-ligand–dependent (*RNF43*–*RSPO*) context observed in *BRAF*-mutant peritoneal metastases [2] and the peritoneal propensity of serrated-type tumors [7]. Our genomic data, however, do not support that framework: *BRAF* mutations were not enriched in peritoneal lesions (7.4%; FDR = 0.298), the *RNF43* trend was non-significant and driven by ovarian samples, *SMAD4* belongs to the TGF-β pathway without a mechanistic link to ligand-dependent WNT signaling, and the single robust signal — *APC* depletion — points, if anything, toward attenuated canonical WNT output. We therefore retain the serrated/WNT-ligand framework only as literature context, not as an interpretation of our data. In the same-subset survival comparison, peritoneal involvement was non-significant both without (HR = 1.38, *P* = 0.181) and with (HR = 1.49, *P* = 0.102) the metastatic-burden term, and the point estimate did not decrease, so the loss of significance relative to the full-cohort model (HR = 1.42, *P* = 0.022) reflects reduced power in the burden-annotated subset rather than an explanation of the signal by burden; the adverse prognostic association of *BRAF* mutation persisted after burden adjustment. These findings complement organotropism signals reported at the copy-number level [8] and at single-cell resolution [28], together supporting a model in which peritoneal colonization is associated with both a permissive stromal–adipocytic niche and an APC-depleted mutational background rather than with either layer alone. The liver findings carry a complementary methodological message: canonical hepatocyte markers (*ALB*, *APOB*, *APOA1*, *HP*) were strongly elevated in liver metastases across RNA-seq cohorts, adjustment for epithelial fraction did not attenuate the liver-tropism set, and 56 of 57 LM_core genes are known liver-function genes whose effect sizes tracked liver specificity (ρ = 0.635) — together indicating that bulk "liver-tropism" programs may largely reflect entrained hepatocytes, a caveat consistent with evidence that organ-specific genes must be excluded before liver-metastasis signatures are derived and that the hepatic microenvironment infiltrates metastatic transcriptomes [9,10], and with the recognized confounding of tumor expression signatures by normal-cell admixture [11]. Read together with the LOCO and permutation analyses, these results suggest that organ-specific expression is substantially shaped at the metastatic site — by niche interactions and, in liver, by admixed resident cells — rather than being fully pre-encoded in the primary tumor.

The increment of this study over prior work can be delineated quantitatively. The CMS4 enrichment is a positive control, re-deriving an association published for the same cohort [6], not a novel finding. Systematic overlap analysis (hypergeometric tests against the universe of genes detectable in each meta-analysis — 14,085 for PM and 9,333 for LM; conclusions unchanged at a universe of 20,000) showed that PM_core overlapped the 366 peritoneal-upregulated genes of Laoukili et al. [6] by 24 of 49 genes (25.1-fold enrichment; *P* = 1.4 × 10⁻²⁸, FDR = 2.0 × 10⁻²⁷); the 61-gene signature of Gelli et al. [12] had only 14 of 40 genes within the detectable universe and therefore no assessable overlap (an underpowered comparison rather than evidence of non-overlap); the 855-gene PM_high set of Lenos et al. [5] overlapped PM_core by 0 genes; and the 805-gene single-cell peritoneal program of Yu et al. [28] overlapped by a single gene (*FABP4*). PM_core therefore represents the cross-cohort meta-analytic convergence core of the same peritoneal signal: 25 of its 49 genes were not captured by the single-cohort analysis of Laoukili et al., and the set shares no overlap with any published predictive signature. To our knowledge, PM_core is the first peritoneal core set supported by triple convergence — paired cross-cohort meta-analysis, a CMS4 positive control, and external-signature enrichment.

Several design features strengthen these inferences. The same-patient, multi-organ paired sampling in GSE190609 removes organ-by-batch and inter-patient confounding that limits comparisons across unrelated cohorts, and patient-aware mixed modeling accounts for within-patient correlation. Analyses were organized around predefined biological hypotheses — a stromal peritoneal niche and an APC-depleted genomic background — rather than post hoc feature selection. The random-effects meta-analysis spanned RNA-seq and microarray platforms with near-complete directional concordance among significant genes (280/282 and 145/146), low peritoneal-program heterogeneity (median I² = 0%), and the transcriptomic findings were independently corroborated at the genomic level in an external large cohort that provided patient-level power (124 patients with versus 1,023 without peritoneal involvement) beyond what the smaller transcriptomic series could offer. Finally, the negative LOCO and permutation-baseline results are reported in full rather than relegated to the supplement; delineating where primary-tumor prediction fails is, in our view, as informative for the field as a positive predictor.

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. All analyses used retrospective public datasets, and the proposed framework warrants prospective validation. All 12 patients in the paired GSE190609 cohort had peritoneal disease, so no liver-only control patients were available; the PM-versus-liver-metastasis contrast may therefore partly reflect differences between patients rather than purely between organs, and the peritoneal meta-analysis included only two cohorts (k = 2), for which REML-based heterogeneity estimates are imprecise and were interpreted descriptively. Hepatocyte admixture was estimated from transcriptional markers rather than direct histological quantification, so its magnitude remains approximate. *BRAF* mutation frequency in peritoneal disease differed between cohorts (32% in GSE190609 vs 7.4% in MSK-MET), a discrepancy likely attributable to cohort selection differences between a peritoneal surgical series and an unselected metastatic population; we therefore did not include *BRAF* in the peritoneal genomic background. MSI, tumor mutational burden, and primary-sidedness annotations in MSK-MET derive from a sample-level snapshot with incomplete coverage, so model 2 (*n* = 302) was underpowered and MSI-high status (2 cases) was not evaluable. Overall survival is defined from the date of sequencing, which occurred a median of 0.9 years (IQR 0.2–2.5; *n* = 1,125) after first metastatic diagnosis; survival estimates are therefore subject to left truncation (immortal-time bias) and should not be extrapolated to time from diagnosis. The apparently protective association of lung involvement (HR = 0.60) reflects sampling selection — all 146 patients with lung involvement had isolated lung metastases, an oligometastatic and potentially resectable population — and should not be interpreted as a biological protective effect. The copy-number analysis (*n* = 177) was underpowered and is reported as exploratory only. The LOCO conclusion itself rests on a sensitivity subset: the primary cross-platform analysis remained nominally significant (AUC = 0.736, *P* = 0.003), non-significance emerged only after restriction to genes detectable on both platforms (AUC = 0.596, *P* = 0.229), and against size- and detection-matched random gene sets only the apparent LM_core row marginally exceeded the null (empirical *P* = 0.031); we therefore frame primary-tumor prediction as not reliably supported at the current sample sizes rather than disproven. Finally, neither the niche imprint nor the genomic background has protein-level, spatial, or experimental confirmation; the two layers provide orthogonal biological support, although causal mechanisms require spatial or experimental validation.

These findings carry no immediate clinical actionability: neither PM_core scores nor *APC* mutation status can currently be used to direct surveillance or therapy. A concrete next step would be a prospective, multicenter cohort with matched primary–metastasis sampling (including peritoneal lesions), spatially resolved and single-cell profiling, and a preregistered analysis plan testing whether *APC* depletion in the primary tumor is associated with subsequent peritoneal recurrence, with primary-sidedness and MSI status as prespecified stratification variables; such a design would directly test the genomic-background hypothesis while localizing the niche imprint to specific cell populations.

In conclusion, metastatic lesions in CRC retain organ-specific transcriptomic niche imprints, and peritoneal spread is associated with an *APC*-depleted genomic background (with a nominal *SMAD4* enrichment that did not survive multiple-testing correction); together these layers form a candidate stratification framework that warrants prospective and experimental validation, for example in cohorts with spatially resolved or single-cell profiling of matched primary and metastatic samples. Two conclusions are negative but, we argue, equally actionable: the primary-tumor transcriptome is not reliably supported as a predictor of metastatic destination, and bulk transcriptomic studies of liver metastases should routinely control for hepatocyte contamination before attributing expression programs to tumor-intrinsic organotropism.
